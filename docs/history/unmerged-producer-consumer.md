# Unmerged work: the producer/consumer split and the HRRR-Zarr screen

Four commits on a local branch (`wip/producer-consumer-zarr`) that never reached `main`. They
were written on 2026-08-31 and 2026-09-01, while the corpus was being generated, and were
built into two images that were on GHCR. The branch was deleted on 2026-09-04 in the public
release. This page is its record. The corpus itself was generated by the image
`7de9dee2a01d`, which predates all four commits.

## What the branch did

### One producer, eight consumers (`bin/run_corpus_machine.py`, +822/−115 against `main`)

In the shipped orchestrator a worker does `pick_hour` (network) and then `get_case` (compute)
for its own day, so every fetch blocked a GPU. Measured on the launch: a sounding took
3 min 59 s with 64 workers fetching at once, against about 22 s serial. The branch moved all
network work into one producer thread and left eight consumers that do all the GPU work and
never touch the network. A day that screens out never enters the queue.

Measured on a stubbed machine with slow consumers: the producer walked all 243 days in
20.9 s, spent 7.3 s fetching, and was parked 13.2 s (63% of its life) on a full queue, peak
exactly 64 of 64. That is the state the split exists to produce. The GPUs are the constraint.

The buffered artifact is the sounding JSON, not the forcing. Running the base-state fit in
the producer was tried and rejected on three measurements. Stage 7c reads the sounding again
for `surface.shtfl_wm2`, so "forcing instead of sounding" is strictly more state. The fit's
representable band [300, 1250] is bit-identical to the band the screen already applied to
the same `HPBL`, so a pre-GPU rejection never fires. And the fit is 1.9 s that costs 8× more
on the one serial thread than on a consumer.

What the split forced, each of which could have gone wrong silently:

- A consumer that returned on an empty queue would exit early once "empty" meant "the
  producer is behind" rather than "no work left", and the reconcile loop would stamp every
  unprocessed day NEVER RAN into a complete, self-consistent, wrong manifest that exits 0.
  Consumers block and exit only on a sentinel, one per consumer, put in a `finally` so a dead
  producer cannot strand them. Verified with a gated fault injection: producer killed at day
  30, consumers drained the 22 queued cases and exited, 243/243 days accounted for, and the
  resume completed to the identical corpus (184 cases / 59 missing) with exactly 30 days
  resumed.
- "not reached" and "NEVER RAN" became different statuses. The first is the ordinary outcome
  of stopping early and is retryable. The second is a day a consumer took and lost, a defect.
  Conflating them had a producer that died at day 30 report 213 failures and hide the real one.
- `wall_s` changed meaning to GPU occupancy alone, where it used to span the screen, the
  sounding and the case. Manifest format 2. Every day has `timing_basis`, because the
  resume merge copies a prior pass's number forward.
- The ETA became a trailing-window rate over completed days rather than one rate over "days
  done", which averaged two unrelated rates.

### The screen reads HRRR-Zarr (`lpdm/hrrrzarr.py`, 236 lines. `bin/test_zarr_screen.py`, 168 lines)

The screen wants three numbers at one grid point, and GRIB charges a full CONUS field per
message: 4.66 MB per candidate hour, and a day that exhausts its pool draws 26 of them.
`hrrrzarr` serves the same analysis fields chunked 150 × 150, so a candidate costs 64–91 kB.
Measured 50–74× less transfer and about 200 ms instead of 1–3 s. The latency is the half that
matters once one thread feeds eight GPUs.

Verified against the repository's own GRIB path over 120 hours spanning 2021–2026:
ACCEPT/REJECT identical 120/120, `SHTFL` bit-exact 120/120, `HPBL` matching its store's width
120/120, no hour within a float16 step of a band edge. The thing scored is
`lpdm.corpus.screen()` itself, imported, never reimplemented. Two rejection *messages* differ
in their last printed digit (`|dz_i/dt|` 19.1 vs 19.2, both far outside 15 %/h). The test
counts that and fails on a changed decision or category, never on text.

**The store's dtype changes part-way through the corpus window.** 2021 and 2023 are `<f2`.
2025 and 2026 are `<f4`. The first version cached the geometry per variable, validated 2021,
and then decoded every 2025 chunk as 45,000 float16s out of a 90,000-byte buffer: a full
chunk of plausible numbers read at the wrong width. The dtype is read from each store's own
`.zarray`, cached per day and never for the life of the process, and the decompressed chunk
length is checked against it independently.

**The sounding stays on GRIB and cannot move.** `hrrrzarr` has no native (hybrid) model
levels. Its `prs` product is 40 pressure levels at 25 mb spacing, so the first level is about
200 m AGL and a 300 m boundary layer contains one point, which cannot locate three
`zStableBottom` breakpoints. And `prs` is 16-bit, giving 0.25 K spacing at 300 K against
theta gradients of 0.01–2 K per level gap.

Measured end to end with only the LES stubbed: sounding fetch 25–26 s against the 3 min 59 s
the launch measured at 64-way. A full 24-hour exhausted day screened in 6.2 s. Buffered
soundings 4506 and 4727 bytes. A retry of two failed days did zero network.

### Documentation commits

Two commits rewrote the deployment guide's §C1b around the architecture rather than the byte
count, added the queue line as the bottleneck indicator, corrected the resume section (a
restart used to re-screen every candidate because `pick_hour` never read an existing
`--json`), and recorded the one-case stubbed-LES check that is now in
[deployment](../les/deployment.md). Verified on the shipped image: dry run 29/29 PASS. Two
real soundings selected and fetched end to end (2021-04-01 15Z and 2021-04-02 20Z, 25.1 s
and 25.3 s, 20 levels, G 10.63 m/s from 352.9°, `shtfl` 198 W/m²), with the resulting records
correctly refused by `check_npz` because `STUB_LES` stamps them stub.

## Why it was not merged

The corpus was complete before the branch was finished, so the throughput problem it solved
no longer needed solving for this project. The branch also sat on the pre-rewrite history (a
history rewrite on `main` had already happened, so the two had no common ancestor), and 320
of its 324 commits were patch-equivalent to commits already on `main`. The four unique
commits are described above in enough detail to re-implement. The code itself is no longer
available. Both images were deleted and the branch was not kept in the offline archive, so this
page is the record.

If the corpus is ever topped up or regenerated, the producer/consumer split and the Zarr
screen are the first things to bring back. They take the network off the GPUs' critical path,
and the per-day screening cost falls by a factor of 50–74.

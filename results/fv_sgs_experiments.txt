  WHAT THIS SETTLES

  The anisotropic split is the physically motivated change -- near a wall, blocking
  suppresses w, and sigma_u:sigma_v:sigma_w ~ 2.5:1.9:1.25 rather than 1:1:1. Applying it
  made the footprint FOUR TIMES worse. That is a real result and it kills the hypothesis:
  the isotropic (2/3)e split was not the error, it was COMPENSATING for one.

  What it was compensating for is measurable. At the receptor the LES delivers

      sigma_w / u* = 1.09   against the neutral surface-layer value of ~1.25

  because at z/Delta ~ 1.5 the eddies that carry w sit at or below the filter scale. A low
  sigma_w makes backward particles descend too slowly, so they travel further before
  touching down -- peak too far upwind, distribution too broad. Both are exactly what the
  baseline shows. The anisotropic split cut sigma_w further (r_w = 0.41), and the error
  grew in the predicted direction and by roughly the predicted amount.

  Supplying the missing variance instead -- scaling so sigma_w matches similarity --
  recovers most of the gap: peak error +86% -> +29%, overlap 36.9% -> 47.6%, 80% area
  45.4 -> 36.4 ha, and the 80% source area pulls in from 3810 m to 2730 m, which also
  relieves the domain-truncation problem. The integral is unchanged at 0.81, so this is
  not a rescaling of the answer.

  So the near-field error at this resolution is a sigma_w DEFICIT, and it is correctable
  in the dispersion model without buying more grid. That reframes the sub-grid gate: the
  gate was measuring resolution correctly, but the consequence of poor resolution turns
  out to be a specific, diagnosable, fixable quantity rather than a diffuse loss.

  WHERE THE TWO CORRECTIONS DIVERGE, AND WHAT THAT MEANS

  Both fix the PEAK identically: 390 -> 270 m, +86% -> +29%. They differ on the tail. The
  tuned scalar reaches 47.6% overlap, the principled surface-layer floor 40.0%, against a
  63.0% floor. The scalar's advantage comes entirely from adding variance ABOVE the surface
  layer, which the MOST floor deliberately will not do.

  Read straight, that says the sigma_w deficit is NOT confined to the surface layer -- the
  LES under-produces vertical variance through much of the boundary layer at this
  resolution. The surface-layer relation can only repair the bottom of it. So:

      the PEAK error is a surface-layer deficit, and is fixable at the closure level;
      the residual TAIL error is boundary-layer-wide, and IS a resolution limitation.

  The scalar is not adopted despite scoring better. It is a constant fitted to one case at
  one height with no rule for transferring it, and adopting it would bake a tuned number
  into the corpus targets.

  HONESTY CONDITIONS. This is a calibration against Monin-Obukhov similarity and is valid
  only where MOST is: flat, uniform, surface layer. It is implemented as a height-dependent
  FLOOR (never reduces the LES's own sub-grid variance, switches itself off above the
  surface layer) so that what transfers to terrain and to other stabilities is the RULE and
  never the number. The scalar x1.349 row above is a diagnostic, not the implementation.
